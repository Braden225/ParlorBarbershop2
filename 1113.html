01The shop

Same avenue. Same chairs. Same question every time.

“What are we doing today?” — and then, unusually for a shop, waiting for the whole answer before picking anything up.

02Where we are

1209 Fort Worth Avenue.

Fort Worth Avenue runs east toward the bridges and downtown, and west into the rest of Oak Cliff. We are on it, in Dallas 75208, which means most of the people sitting in these chairs got here in under ten minutes and will be back in two to six weeks depending on what they asked for last time.

That proximity changes how a shop works. When your customers are your neighbors, a bad cut is not an anonymous transaction that disappears into a city — it is somebody you will see at the shop on the corner on Thursday. It is a good discipline. It is why we would rather spend two minutes at the start asking questions than five minutes at the end apologizing.

The shop sounds like a barbershop, because it is one. Clippers running, the door going, two or three conversations overlapping, and somebody’s kid in the second chair being told for the fourth time to keep their head straight. Nobody here is trying to make it feel like a spa, and nobody has asked us to.

What we care about is narrower than atmosphere. It is whether the line is right, whether the neckline is square, and whether you can still see the shape of the cut in two weeks. Everything else is decoration.

03Oak Cliff

Six barbers, one Saturday, no charge.

In August 2018, Vogel Alcove reported that six barbers from The Parlor gave free back-to-school haircuts to children and parents the organization serves. Vogel Alcove works with Dallas families experiencing homelessness, and a haircut before the first day of school is not a small thing when you are eight years old and everyone is looking at you.

We are putting it here for the reason it is worth putting anywhere: it happened, and it is on the record. It was a single event in 2018, not a program that runs on a schedule, and we would rather say that plainly than let a good day do more work than it earned.

05How we work

Four things we hold to.

  1. 1

    The conversation comes before the clippers.

    Two minutes standing at the mirror is the cheapest insurance either of us will ever buy.

  2. 2

    You hear the truth about your hair.

    Including the parts you would rather not: the crown that will not lie down, the growth pattern fighting the part you want, the beard patch that is not coming in. Better said now than discovered at home.

  3. 3

    Nothing gets added quietly.

    The number you are told at booking is the number at the end. If something changes, you agree to it first, out loud.

  4. 4

    Speak up while you are still in the chair.

    Thirty seconds of adjustment beats two weeks of quietly regretting it. We would genuinely rather you said something.

A shop is only as good as the last neckline it squared off.
